Testing and verification: The inspected validation evidence records 20 independent closed-form prismatic-beam comparisons passing within their stated tolerances. Engineering methodology: Identify governing equations, assumptions and boundary conditions; derive the calculation sequence; implement it; solve representative problems independently; compare benchmarks; then correct and retest discrepancies. Known limitations: Public beta under active development and verification. Experimental and unsupported cases remain, including gaps recorded for support settlement and equation-load workflows. Development roadmap: Expand systematic tests and validate individual modules, improve calculation traceability and refine engineering reports before treating outputs as dependable design tools.
Lessons from the work: A functioning interface is insufficient evidence of engineering correctness. Explicit assumptions, independent solutions and a reviewable calculation trail are central to a credible engineering tool.

CAD-to-BOQ Estimation Automation
Reviewed DXF mappings connect drawing quantities to Excel BOQ workflows, with explicit units and traceable extraction evidence.
Project overview: A civil engineering and quantity-surveying automation project connecting reviewed drawing inputs, engineering quantity calculations and an Excel bill of quantities.
Problem and objectives: Manual transfer between drawings, schedules and spreadsheets creates repeated entry and makes quantity origins difficult to trace. The objective is to reduce that transfer while retaining explicit engineering review of assumptions and mappings.
Technology and architecture: Python and ezdxf read CAD entities through explicit mappings. Workbook adapters connect quantities to an Excel estimation system, with browser interfaces for BOQ review. The workflow keeps drawing interpretation, calculation, spreadsheet recalculation and release checks as separate responsibilities.
Implemented in the inspected source: Mapped DXF entity counts, straight-line lengths and closed straight-polyline areas; explicit unit conversion; reviewed target-cell mappings; extraction evidence and drawing hashes. Additional workflows cover footing/member inputs, concrete and reinforcement calculations, stock-length optimization and workbook summaries. Those paths retain assumptions and input requirements.
Tested in an isolated sample: The actual extraction module processed a new synthetic millimetre-unit DXF: a 6 m beam line, a 6 m² slab outline and four column markers. The outputs matched 6, 6 and 4. An open polyline requested as an area was rejected. This narrow test does not validate a complete building estimate.
Recorded verification and release state: Repository status records workbook reconciliation and alternate-engine checks. Native Excel validation remains pending in that record. The project handoff also identifies newer estimation interface work; the installed workbook baseline should not be mistaken for a finished end-to-end release.
Screenshot evidence: The screenshot uses the actual read-only BOQ viewer with a newly created synthetic workbook. Rates and totals are demonstration values only. No client drawing, estimate or source workbook was copied into the portfolio.
Known limitations: Extraction requires reviewed mappings, supported geometry and declared units. It does not infer a complete BOQ from arbitrary CAD. Curved geometry and incomplete schedules require review. Revised inputs, rates and native Excel recalculation/release validation remain prerequisites for a final estimate. File preflight alone is not takeoff validation.
Deployed status: No public estimation deployment was verified. The portfolio presents local development evidence rather than a production estimating service.
Development roadmap: Complete reviewed input mappings and current member workflows, expand independent quantity comparisons, resolve native Excel validation and strengthen drawing-to-quantity-to-cost traceability before final release.
Lessons from the work: Automation is most useful when the origin, unit and assumption behind each quantity remain inspectable. A successful script run and a reconciled spreadsheet are distinct from an approved engineering estimate.
